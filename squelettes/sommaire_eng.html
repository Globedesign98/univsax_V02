Welcome to the European University of Saxophone

Since its creation, the European University of Saxophone has helped promote saxophone performance at the highest level.

Artistic Director Mr Claude DELANGLE and his teaching team—made up of some of the most renowned French pedagogues as well as leading saxophone personalities—have ensured the free circulation of expertise and knowledge across Europe and internationally.

In collaboration with the city of Gap and with the support of VANDOREN and SELMER, the University has an exciting programme in store for you. Throughout the course, from Monday 13 July to Thursday 23 July 2026 inclusive, you will be able to attend the various concerts featuring students as well as their teachers.

Overview

The European University of Saxophone takes place over 11 full days, during the second half of July.

Upon arriving in Gap, the 54 registered students are welcomed at the youth residence (see map) by the entire staff: organisers, teachers and pianists, local representatives, etc.

A welcome reception gives them the opportunity to meet, get to know the other students, and talk with the teachers and organisers. The organisers then go over all practical details (course timetable, daily schedule, teaching, concert programme, etc.). Students are accompanied by a dedicated staff member throughout the entire course. This person is their direct contact in case of questions or issues.

Teaching

Over the past years, the teaching team of the European University of Saxophone in Gap has been delighted to welcome around fifty students, whose overall standard is fairly homogeneous. This very positive reality allows the teachers to further develop their teaching methods towards greater versatility.

Individual lessons naturally remain the core of this teaching and give every student the opportunity to work with each teacher. They are now complemented by many other activities. Participants can thus appreciate the team both as teachers and as artists. Beyond instrumental improvement itself, the activities offered include:

  • The opportunity for each student to rehearse individually with the two pianists (Fumie Ito and Iren Seleljo)
  • Masterclasses
  • Saxophone ensemble
  • Competition preparation
  • Pedagogy classes
  • Concert preparation

All students must have prepared a programme of at least 30 minutes before the start of the University.

Please note that using photocopies for sheet music is strictly prohibited.

History

In 1989, Serge BICHON (Professor at the Conservatoire National de Région de Lyon), Claude DELANGLE (Professor at the Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique de Paris) and Yves RAMBAUD (Professor at the École Nationale de Musique de Gap) founded the European University of Saxophone, thanks to the financial support of the municipality of Gap.

Their goals were multiple: to affirm the saxophone's place as a modern instrument that is also fully classical; to improve students' training; and to actively foster (ahead of its time!) the free circulation of knowledge and expertise within a Europe still in the making. Finally, this project also aimed to enrich the cultural programme of the Hautes-Alpes. To that end, they called upon the best saxophone teachers in France and decided each year to complete the team with a European professor—thus giving the University its "European" dimension.

Over the previous 35 editions, students have had the opportunity to refine their instrumental mastery with teachers from Spain, Switzerland, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, etc.

Initially designed for French students, the University quickly evolved with the times and has for many years welcomed students from all around the world. Japanese, Chinese, Australian, American, Russian musicians—and of course students from all countries of the European Union—now make up the majority of participants, coming to absorb the French technique and style, widely acclaimed worldwide.

The first generation of students has now become professional, progressively exporting the "Gap model" throughout the world. While the concept has been adopted in various places across Europe, Gap continues to lead thanks to its top-level teaching team, innovations introduced year after year, and the spectacular setting of the Hautes-Alpes—along with the unique atmosphere that prevails during the course. The European University of Saxophone in Gap has thus become a must for professional saxophonists worldwide.

A day at UES

7:30: After a night at the youth residence, the 54 students are invited to breakfast in the facility's restaurant. A meal you should not miss, as the day is often long and intense.

8:45 to 12:45: The 54 participants are divided into 9 groups of 6 students. Over the entire course, each student will spend 40 minutes with each of the 7 teachers, as well as with the two accompanying pianists. All students must have prepared at least a 30-minute programme in advance, which they will be able to work on with every member of the teaching team. During the rest of the morning, students have access to many practice rooms. They can also test products presented by the manufacturers Selmer and Vandoren, who are present on site for a few days (typically 2 to 3 full days), and have their instrument serviced by the repairer from JS Musique, also on site.

13:00: Students have lunch at the residence restaurant. Teachers are often present as well.

AFTERNOON: The beginning of the afternoon is generally devoted to rehearsals with quartets and the various ensembles formed by the teachers from the first day, according to students' wishes and standard (this is the group setting in which the students will perform their final concert at the end of the course). The rest of the afternoon is mainly dedicated to public classes given by some of the teachers, as well as by renowned guest artists. Finally, students can enjoy the many tourist attractions of the city of Gap...

19:30 or 20:30: Students have dinner at the residence restaurant and get ready for the last event of the day, always highly anticipated: either the scheduled concert or a free evening...

18:30 or 21:00: In turn, and accompanied by the renowned pianists of the European University of Saxophone, each teacher performs in concert at the Chapelle des Pénitents, in the very centre of Gap. They give high-quality, free concerts for the students and for the approximately 200 audience members present every night.

Claude Delangle

Claude Delangle has been teaching saxophone at the Paris Conservatoire (CNSMDP) since September 1988, the year he founded the European University of Saxophone in Gap, of which he is the Artistic Director.

At Pierre Boulez's request, he performed as a soloist and as a member of the Ensemble Intercontemporain from 1986 to 2000. He worked closely with Luciano Berio, who wrote the saxophone concerto Chemin VII – Récit for him, and performed alongside him at the Norton Lectures at Harvard University, London's Queen Elizabeth Hall, New York's Tisch Center, the Cologne Philharmonie, with the BBC Symphony, among others.

His stage projects have deeply shaped his approach to bringing contemporary creation to life. Creating and teaching are at the heart of his musical activity.

Claude Delangle has recorded twelve albums for BIS and has participated in monographic recordings of composers including Claude Debussy, Anton Webern, Luciano Berio, Edison Denisov, Hugues Dufourt, Gérard Grisey and Philippe Leroux, for Deutsche Grammophon, Harmonia Mundi, Erato and Verany.

For four decades, he has performed with his wife, pianist Odile Catelin-Delangle, professor at the École Normale de Musique de Paris.

Claude Delangle contributes to the development of prototypes for Henri Selmer Paris and directs a collection at Éditions Lemoine (Paris).

Claude Delangle is a Knight of the Order of Arts and Letters.

Nicolas Arsenijevic

Nicolas Arsenijevic is one of the most acclaimed saxophonists of his generation. A graduate of the Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique de Paris in 2016 (Bachelor's and Master's) in Claude Delangle's class, he has for several years moved between contemporary creation, the original saxophone repertoire, transcriptions, Balkan traditional music and musical theatre.

Joonatan Rautiola

Born in Helsinki, Finland in 1983, Joonatan Rautiola studied saxophone at the Sibelius Academy with Pekka Savijoki and at the Paris Conservatoire with Claude Delangle. Winner of major international competitions (Dinant, Düsseldorf, Oslo, Nova Gorica, Paris), he has performed as a soloist with, among others, the Strasbourg Philharmonic Orchestra, the Latvian National Symphony Orchestra, the Finnish Radio Symphony Orchestra and the Düsseldorf Symfoniker, and has given recitals in London, Dublin, Saint Petersburg, Tokyo and at Carnegie Hall in New York. He teaches saxophone at the Sibelius Academy and gives public classes in London, Tokyo, Strasbourg and Riga.

Eva van Grinsven



With her broad palette of tone colours, her captivating stage presence and the tremendous joy she displays while playing, saxophonist Eva van Grinsven has won the hearts of both press and public time and time again in the last years.

Her busy and multifaceted performance practice often centres around chamber music, but Eva also performs as a solo artist (a.o. with Liepaja Symphony Orchestra, PHION, Dutch Youth String Orchestra) and with various music companies, including the Dutch Radio Philharmonic Orchestra and the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra. But no matter what the musical setting or style may be, to Eva the story behind the notes always comes first.

In 2008, Eva graduated with honour from the Amsterdam Conservatory, where she studied with Arno Bornkamp. Also she took lessons with Professor Daniel Gauthier at the Hochschule für Musik und Tanz in Cologne. In that same year, her duo Sax & Stix won the Vriendenkrans, a prestigious prize that is awarded to promising young musicians every year by the Dutch Society for Friends of the Concertgebouw and the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra. Many prizes and awards would follow.

With the Berlage Saxophone Quartet, Eva received both the Jury and the Public Award from the Dutch Classical Talent Award 2013 and the Kersjesprijs 2015, amongst others.

Both Sax & Stix's debut album and the three cd's of the Berlage Saxophone Quartet received jubilant reviews from both the Dutch and the international music press. Her passion for 1920s and 1930s saxophone music also led to performances and album recordings with the Dudok Quartet (Busch Quintett) and Café Dansant (with soprano singer & pianist). Since 2014 she forms a duo with pianist Helena Basilova, with whom she released in 2016 the album ¨Rendez-vous Russe¨, winning the prestigious German Echo Klassik Prize in 2017. Eva teaches classical saxophone at Conservatorium Maastricht since 2016. She has been invited to teach masterclasses at the conservatories of Bruxelles, Antwerp, Liège, Versailles, Würzburg, Osnabrück, Sevilla, Cordoba, Jaén, Palma di Mallorca, Riga, Innsbruck and Vienna.
